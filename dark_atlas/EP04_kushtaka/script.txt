In Alaska, if you hear a baby crying down by the water at night, far from any house... don't go looking for it.

Because there is no baby. Something out there is using its voice.

They call it the Kushtaka. The land otter man.

For generations, the Tlingit people of Southeast Alaska have told stories about it. Over a century ago, an ethnologist traveled there and wrote those stories down.

According to the legend, the Kushtaka can take any shape. A drowned friend. A lost child. Even your own mother, calling your name from the beach.

But here's the terrifying part. If it catches you, it doesn't kill you. It slowly turns you into one of them.

They say only a shaman could bring a person back. Without one, you stay on that shore forever, crying for the next traveler.

So if you're ever alone on that coast tonight, and the crying moves closer to the waves, don't answer it. Don't follow it.

Because it isn't lost. It's waiting for you to come find it.
