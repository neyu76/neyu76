In Pennsylvania, if a man in a ragged fur coat walks into your house a few nights before Christmas and throws candy across the floor... don't grab it.

Because he isn't giving you a gift. He's testing you.

They call him the Belsnickel.

German families brought him to Pennsylvania two hundred years ago. By 1827, a Philadelphia newspaper was already describing him.

According to the legend, he comes before Santa, his face dark with soot, a bundle of switches in his hand. He asks the children if they've been good. Then he scatters the candy and waits.

But here's the terrifying part. He never tells you what good means. He only watches who moves first... and who reaches the fastest.

They say the only safe thing is to wait until he tells you to take it. The children who rush feel the switch.

So if you hear heavy boots on the porch this December, and something hits your floor like falling sweets, don't reach for it.

Because the Belsnickel isn't counting your presents. He's counting your hands.
