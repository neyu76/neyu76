In New Mexico, if you walk home alone at night, and black wool is lying at the crossroads... don't cross it.

Because that isn't wool. And it only waits for people who are alone.

They call her La Mala Hora. The Evil Hour.

In 1910, a folklorist recording Spanish folklore in New Mexico wrote about her. He described a spirit that haunts dark crossroads, terrorizing people who walk alone at night. Most of the time, it looks like a lock of wool. Sometimes, a whole sheep's fleece.

According to the legend, she can also become a woman in black.

But here's the terrifying part. She isn't there for you.

They say if you see her at the crossroads, someone you love will die soon. She doesn't chase you. She doesn't make a sound. She just watches you walk home... to see which door you open.

So if you're ever on a dark New Mexico road tonight, don't cross alone.

Because that wool isn't lying in the road by accident. It's waiting to see who you'll lose.
