# 05 — Dialogue, Slang & Hooks

The voice of the channel. Short, punchy, meme-aware, emotionally direct.

---

## Dialogue principles

1. **Every line does a job:** advance plot, reveal character, or twist the knife. Cut anything that just "sounds nice."
2. **Short sentences.** 4-10 words. People scrolling don't parse clauses.
3. **Subtext is optional, clarity is mandatory.** The audience must always know who's winning and who's losing.
4. **Repetition = motif.** Reuse a catchphrase across parts ("Almost done, son."). Repetition is what makes it feel authored.
5. **Villains brag, heroes endure.** Give the villain the cocky lines (they're meme-able), give the hero quiet, loaded ones.
6. **The innocent gets the spine-tingling line.** The child's vow / testimony is the emotional peak — make those lines land.

---

## Internet-slang kit (Gen Z / Gen Alpha)

Sprinkle, don't drown. 1-3 slang hits per part is plenty. Use them as the
villain's flex or as the reversal at the end.

- **mogged / mogged back** — dominated / out-classed (and then out-classing back).
- **loser / L / took an L** — the core insult to be reversed.
- **alpha / sigma / "the little guy"** — villain's power flex / belittling.
- **it's giving [X]** — describing a vibe.
- **cooked / done / finished** — they're in trouble.
- **the audacity / the disrespect** — reaction beat.
- **caught in 4K** — exposed with proof.
- **spill the tea / tipped them off** — the reveal/snitch beat.
- **glow-up** — the hero's rebirth.

> The signature reversal: the villain's insult in Part 1 ("You got mogged,
> loser") becomes the hero's silent flex in the finale (the loser now owns
> everything). **Plant the insult early; pay it off with no words later.**

## Reusable line templates (fill the [brackets])

- Put-down (villain): "You're a loser, [hero]. You always were."
- Belittle: "Nice [thing]. Did you build it yourself? That's cute."
- Power flex: "You built it on *my* [land] now, little guy."
- Hero's promise (catchphrase candidate): "Almost done, [son/kid]."
- Hero's vow to stay: "I'm not going anywhere."
- Innocent's turn: "You took my dad's [X]. I'm gonna take everything you have."
- Karma trigger: "We found [evidence]. Your prints are all over it."
- Betrayer's exit: "Move. This doesn't concern you."
- Finale gut-punch: "Who bought it?!" / "Some [hero-animal]. He said his name was [Hero]."

---

## Hook & title formula

- **Episode titles:** 1-3 words, noun-heavy, ominous: "The Cruel Eviction", "The
  Demolition", "The Midnight Heist", "The Golden Lot". Title = mini-hook.
- **Series title:** short, rhythmic, hints at the loneliness/struggle: "Dog
  Swings Alone". Pattern: `[Hero] [verb] Alone` or `[Hero]'s [Promise Object]`.
- **Caption hook (pinned/first comment):** pose the injustice as a question:
  "Would you forgive her?" / "He built it with his own hands. Then they took it."

---

## Voiceover direction

- One narrator-free style: all dialogue, no third-person narrator. Characters
  carry it.
- Distinct voice per role: Hero = warm/weary, Tyrant = smooth/smug, Innocent =
  small/earnest, Justice = flat/calm, Betrayer = cold/clipped.
- Keep VO slightly faster than natural; trim breaths in the edit to hit pace.
