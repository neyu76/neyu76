# 01 — The Story Engine

The repeatable narrative formula behind every series on this channel.
This is animal-agnostic: it works whether your hero is a dog, a beaver, an ox,
a sparrow, or an elephant. Swap the cast, keep the engine.

---

## The one-sentence promise

> A good, underestimated character is stripped of everything by an arrogant,
> powerful one — endures humiliation — and then karma + a clever twist hands
> them total victory while the villain loses everything.

This is **rags -> ruin -> revenge -> rebirth**. It is the most reliable
emotional engine on short-form video because it pays off our deepest cravings:
**justice, fairness, and the underdog winning.**

---

## The 7-Beat Arc (the spine)

Every series is 6-8 parts. Map them to these beats. (Combine beats if you do 6.)

| # | Beat | What happens | Emotion we farm | Ends on |
|---|------|--------------|-----------------|---------|
| 1 | **LOSS** | Hero is humiliated and abandoned/robbed of a loved one or status | Sympathy + outrage | The hero left with nothing but a promise |
| 2 | **INJUSTICE** | Villain uses money/power/law to take the hero's last thing (home, land, job, child) | Anger | The system fails the hero |
| 3 | **ENDURANCE** | Hero hits rock bottom, forced into humiliating labor; an ally (often the child) silently vows revenge | Heartbreak + simmering hope | "I'm going to take everything you have." |
| 4 | **AWAKENING** | The wronged side gains a secret weapon — knowledge, evidence, a witness, a hidden asset | Anticipation | A trap is set / a call is made |
| 5 | **KARMA** | The villain's own crime/greed exposes them; arrest, downfall, public shame | Catharsis (round 1) | Villain caught; hero's role hidden |
| 6 | **REBIRTH** | Hero is rewarded — money, status, a glow-up ("mogs back") — through honesty/luck/work | Triumph | Hero and villain's paths cross; roles reversed |
| 7 | **ULTIMATE REVENGE** | Reunion + the twist: the villain learns the "loser" they mocked now owns the very thing they lost | Catharsis (round 2) + awe | The villain says the hero's name in despair |

**Note how "Dog Swings Alone" maps exactly onto this** (see
`/reference/dog-swings-alone-breakdown.md`). That's the template, not a one-off.

---

## The 5 emotional levers (pull at least 3 per series)

1. **Injustice** — the audience must feel "this is NOT fair."
2. **A protected innocent** — a child/elderly/disabled character whose suffering is unbearable (the emotional anchor).
3. **Betrayal** — someone the hero trusted switches sides for money/status.
4. **Karma / poetic justice** — the punishment must *rhyme* with the crime.
5. **The reversal ("mog back")** — the exact insult thrown at the hero gets turned around at the end.

The single most powerful one is **poetic justice**: the villain must lose the
*specific* thing they stole, *to the specific person* they mocked. In the source
series, Wolf loses the gold buried under the very lot he took from Dog — to Dog.
**Always plant this payload early (the gold under the lot) and detonate it last.**

---

## The "Promise & Payoff" loop (your retention weapon)

Plant a small, concrete **promise object** in Part 1 and pay it off in the
finale. In the source series it's the **swing set** + the line **"Almost done, son."**

- Part 1: promise made (build the swing / say the line).
- Mid: promise broken/destroyed (swing torn down).
- Finale: promise kept (swing rebuilt) — same line, now fulfilled.

This recurring motif is what makes a series feel *authored* instead of random.
**Every new series needs its own promise object + catchphrase.** Examples you can reuse:
a half-finished boat, a recipe book, a music box, a vegetable garden, a kite, a
wedding ring, a school report card.

---

## Structural rules that keep it bingeable

- **One clear hero, one clear villain.** No moral ambiguity. The audience must know exactly who to root for and who to hate.
- **The child/innocent is the secret protagonist.** They often drive the turn (the tip-off, the testimony). They make the revenge righteous, not cruel.
- **Money = power = morality test.** Wealth corrupts the villains; honesty + work redeems the hero. Keep this clean and obvious.
- **Every part ends on a cliffhanger.** Never resolve and relax inside a part.
- **The villain is never *just* lucky — they're greedy.** Their downfall must come from their own crime, so karma feels earned.

---

## Anti-patterns (why most copycats flop)

- ❌ Random comedy clips with no through-line (no compounding, no playlist binge).
- ❌ Sympathetic/complex villain — kills the catharsis.
- ❌ Hero wins by luck alone — feels cheap; pair luck with virtue + the villain's greed.
- ❌ Slow openings — if the injustice isn't landing in the first 2 seconds, viewers swipe.
- ❌ No recurring motif — the series feels disposable.
- ❌ Resolving the revenge too early — spread catharsis across two waves (Karma in P5, Ultimate Revenge in P7).

---

## Series-length variants

- **6 parts (lean):** merge LOSS+INJUSTICE into Part 1, and KARMA+REBIRTH if needed.
- **7 parts (standard):** the source-series structure above.
- **8-10 parts (saga):** add a "false hope" beat (hero almost recovers, then a second betrayal) between ENDURANCE and AWAKENING, and a "villain doubles down" beat before KARMA.
