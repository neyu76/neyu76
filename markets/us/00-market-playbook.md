# US Market Playbook — Sad-Story Human Drama (United States · en-US edition)

Localization layer that pivots the German "@heartwarming_stories4" photoreal
tearjerker engine to the **United States market**. The story engine (3-party
hook, 5-Act, two-track audio, hero prop, cliffhanger) is UNCHANGED — this
playbook only swaps **language to American English (en-US)**, names, idioms,
settings, music, hashtags, posting calendar, monetization, and channel setup.
The US sad-story skills + master prompt reference this file.

```
topic-sad-story-drama (20 locked SERIES BRIEFS, en-US)
  -> script-sad-story-drama (adopt verbatim -> 2-layer script + handoff, English)
     -> production-prompts/MASTER-PROMPT-sad-story-drama.md
        (Asset Bank · seed images · Seedance/KLING silent b-roll · Veo Omni 3-party HOOK lip-sync · narrator · EN/VI review)
```

> **Genre is PHOTOREAL HUMANS, not animals.** Cast = real-looking people:
> abandoned/sold/orphaned children and cast-out elders, a cold family betrayer,
> a kind rescuer, a mystery figure. This is the human-drama cousin of the
> repo's animal-drama saga — same emotional engine, photoreal execution.

---

## 0. WHY THIS FORMAT WINS IN THE US (the thesis)
The German channel proved a **serialized AI tearjerker** loop: a protected
innocent is wronged by family in cruel weather, endures, is rescued, and every
part cuts on a cliffhanger that farms "Part 2!" comments. America has the same
hardwiring — but it is a **bigger, faster, more saturated market**, so the US
edition leans on three native advantages:

- **Outrage + protective instinct travel zero-friction in English.** A child
  mistreated by a parent triggers comment-section moral outrage ("how could a
  mother DO that?") in every US demographic. That outrage is the share engine.
- **The micro-drama (ReelShort / DramaBox) boom** already trained tens of
  millions of US viewers — especially women 35-65 — to binge serialized
  melodrama with cliffhangers. We serve that craving in a free, muted-friendly,
  AI-photoreal short.
- **"Part 2!" comment farming is a pure-English retention machine.** Cut on the
  worst second; the audience finishes the story for you in the comments.

Cultural pillars to lean on:
- **Family is sacred** — a parent betraying a child = maximum emotional pull.
- **The underdog + second chances** — the wronged kid who is later vindicated is
  the core American story (rags-to-redemption).
- **Rich-vs-poor injustice** — designer coat vs. broken zipper reads instantly.
- **Faith / "everything happens for a reason"** — the kind-stranger rescue and
  the karma turn resonate with a huge slice of the US audience.

## 1. LANGUAGE STRATEGY
- **Primary (and only spoken language): `en-US`** — natural, plain American
  English. Broadest reach on US TikTok / Reels / Shorts.
- Spoken language for the HOOK dialogue AND the narrator voiceover = en-US.
  Vietnamese appears ONLY in the Phase-6 review as a gloss for the editor.
- **Register:** conversational, emotional, never stiff/formal. The narrator is
  warm, weary, slow. Betrayers are cold and curt. The child gets one
  spine-tingling line. **No internet slang** (wrong tone for the 35-65 core
  audience) — keep it timeless and plain.
- **WPM:** English is tighter than German — target the same **140-150 WPM**
  band for narration; emotional-peak beats drop to ~30 WPM or full silence
  (SFX + score carry them). Hook lines stay 3-8 words; narration lines ≤ ~12.
- **Numbers in spoken lines:** spell out numbers under one hundred (TTS-safe).

## 2. US CAST CASTING TABLE (photoreal humans, American film look)
Cast realistic American people. The look is **cinematic US/North-American film**
(natural skin texture, 35mm feel, shallow DOF) — never cartoon, never stylized.

| Role | Best US casting | Reads as |
|------|-----------------|----------|
| **VICTIM** (protected innocent) | child 5-10 (boy or girl) OR elder 70+; ragged/ill-fitting clothes + **one bright-color item**; clutching a hero prop | pure, vulnerable, must-protect |
| **BETRAYER / AGGRESSOR** (family) | mother / father / stepmother / stepfather / grown child; **elegant, expensive wardrobe** (designer coat, manicured) = rich-poor contrast | cold, entitled, cruel |
| **THIRD-PARTY** (hook amplifier — pick one) | **CO-VICTIM** (frail grandmother / baby / younger sibling) · **COMPLICIT WITNESS** (the other parent looking away, holding a new baby) · **TRANSACTIONAL STRANGER** (the buyer / landlord taking cash or keys) | doubles protection / doubles betrayal / makes it a sale |
| **ANTAGONIST** (optional / may be the stranger) | rough buyer, cruel landlord, predatory stepfather | deepens the danger |
| **RESCUER** | diner owner, kind waitress, grandmother, soft-spoken stranger; **warm, worn-kind** | the first warmth |
| **MYSTERY** (cliffhanger figure) | man in a dark coat, a returning parent, a stranger with a folder/keys; alignment **unknown** | the "Part 2!" engine |
| **NARRATOR** (carries the body) | one off-screen American voice — warm, weary, slow, sorrowful | the storyteller |

Rules (unchanged): one clear victim / one clear betrayer, zero ambiguity; never
use one person for two roles; ≤ 15 total assets per project; the **HOOK must
field at least 3 interacting characters** (aggressor + victim + third party).

> Signature anchor: a **recurring hero prop** (a worn teddy bear, an old photo,
> a too-small pair of shoes) is the channel's emotional fingerprint — plant it
> in the first 10 seconds, threaten it mid-video, return it at the cliffhanger.

## 4. IDIOM / MORAL-OUTRAGE KIT (replaces German lines)
The share engine is **moral outrage in plain English** — short, brutal, quotable.

- **Aggressor's cruel command (Beat 1):** "Get out. Now." · "He's not my problem
  anymore." · "Take him. One month." · "This house is mine now."
- **The label to reverse (planted Part 1 → reversed in the finale):** "a mistake"
  · "worthless" · "nobody's child" · "dead weight." The whole series exists to
  flip that label.
- **Moral-outrage trigger line (narrator or caption — the comment bait):** "Her
  coat cost more than his whole life." · "She didn't even look back." · "Three
  days after the funeral, a stranger took his hand."
- **The child's single spine-tingling line (Beat 2):** "Please don't leave me." ·
  "Can I come with you?" · "I'll be good, I promise."
- **The finale "button" (label reversed):** the betrayer says the victim's name;
  the "mistake" is the one who saved them. Plain, no slang.

> Avoid US internet slang entirely. The 35-65 core audience wants sincere
> melodrama, not memes. Keep every line timeless.

## 5. SETTINGS / WORLD FLAVORS (pick one per series — American texture)
Cool palette dominates (blue-grey, desaturated); the ONE warm point is the place
the victim is locked out of (a lit window seen from the cold street).

- **Greyhound-style bus station, night, rain** — grade: cold blue + sodium-amber.
- **Snowy small-town cemetery / churchyard** — grade: white-grey, no warmth.
- **Run-down apartment hallway / project stairwell** — grade: dim, sickly green.
- **Rust-belt winter street, row houses, lit windows** — grade: cold blue, one warm window.
- **Roadside diner at night (rescue)** — grade: warm amber inside vs. cold rain outside.
- **Highway underpass / shelter** — grade: harsh sodium light, wet concrete.
- **Trailer park / motel in the cold** — grade: grey overcast, neon "VACANCY".

Pathetic fallacy is mandatory: stage every abandonment under **snow, rain, or a
grey sky**. Grade-by-emotion is unchanged (cold blue-grey for
danger/abandonment, warm amber for memory/rescue, cold + one warm point for the
cliffhanger).

## 6. MUSIC FLAVOR (emotional, American)
- Loss / abandonment: solo piano, sustained low strings, distant wind.
- Tension / dread: low drone + a single ticking/heartbeat pulse.
- Rescue / warmth: warm strings + soft acoustic guitar.
- Cliffhanger: a held dissonant string + sudden cut to silence.
- Emotion first; keep it cinematic-orchestral, never pop, never licensed-vocal.

## 7. UPLOAD / HASHTAGS / POSTING (US)
- Title + caption in English; pose the injustice as a **question** to bait comments.
- **Hashtags (mix broad + niche):** `#heartbreaking #sadstory #emotional #part1
  #fyp #foryou #family #truestory #cryingtiktok #storytime #aishortfilm`
- **AI disclosure:** label every video as AI-generated per platform rules
  (TikTok "AI-generated content" toggle / a visible "AI" note). Non-negotiable
  in the US — undisclosed AI risks distribution penalties and trust loss.
- **Posting calendar hooks:** Mother's Day (May), Father's Day (June),
  Thanksgiving, Christmas, "back to school" (Aug-Sep), the first cold snap /
  first snow (drives the winter-abandonment theme).
- **Cadence:** batch a FULL series before posting Part 1 (never break a
  cliffhanger streak); post daily or every other day at a fixed US time
  (e.g., 7-9pm ET); pin Part 1; one playlist per series; first comment teases
  the next part; reply to early comments to boost reach.

## 8. MONETIZATION (US-appropriate — avoid the German channel's mistakes)
The source channel begged for PayPal donations from a personal email — which
broke trust once viewers saw it was run from overseas. For the US, use clean,
native monetization instead:

- **TikTok Creator Rewards / Reels Play / YouTube Shorts** monetization once eligible.
- **A playlist + Series paywall** (TikTok Series) for binge episodes.
- **Merch** tied to the hero prop (the teddy bear, the pendant) once a series hits.
- **Brand-safe sponsorships** (family, insurance, charity) — the wholesome-sad
  tone is advertiser-friendly IF the AI label is clear and content stays tasteful.
- **No personal donation-guilt loop.** If you ask for support, do it through a
  platform-native tip jar with a transparent creator identity.

---

## 9. CONTENT SAFETY GUARDRAILS (critical for this genre in the US)
This genre depicts children in distress, so stay strictly on the right side:
- **Fictional characters only.** No real people, no real names, no real cases.
- **No graphic harm, no sexualization, never exploitation** — the cruelty is
  emotional/neglect (cold, hunger, being sent away), shown tastefully, never abuse.
- **Hope is mandatory** — the rescuer and the karma/reunion arc keep it
  uplifting, not misery for its own sake.
- **Always disclose AI.** See §7.
